# Shaped maps, arenas, cities, and carved worlds

Read this reference when the concept has a recognizable form or a deliberate route graph. The catalog below records the implementation and its design comments at the time of this skill's creation; it is a source review, not a claim that these generators passed new playtests. Follow the linked code when controls or revisions change. Opening comments sometimes describe an earlier design: Anthill and Canals now have **no home pond**, despite older introductory wording.

## Coverage and useful precedents

Each row covers that generator's `.cpp` and matching options `.h`. “Repeated wedge” means a shared continuous design rotated around the centre; it does **not** promise exact integer-tile, resource-amount, path-length, or economic symmetry. Exact orbit invariance is separately enforced by Symmetric arena.

## Translate a visual idea into a game

Write the intended **player decision** first, then its terrain mechanism and its failure condition. These implemented examples illustrate the method:

- **“Expand through chambers.”** Anthill gives the queen room a finite dry kit and puts renewal in other chambers. Its stone fixes the room supply. The failure is an AI unable to leave, a queen without construction room, or a farm without water.
- **“Besiege your neighbour while the other besieges you.”** Carousel places each elbow next to the next home through thin stone, but makes the walking invasion route go through the centre. The failure is a lane so wide the towers cannot cover it, or a shortcut around the wall.
- **“Fight over banks before swimming changes the routes.”** Canals separates weapon reach from unit reach. Channel width is an opening-timing control. The failure is a diagonal water gap that units walk through immediately, or towers with no meaningful coverage.
- **“Choose between a safe home economy and a rich frontier.”** City states gives homes renewal and space while increasing commons rewards toward the heart. The failure is a commons with no worthwhile prize, or one so fertile it becomes impassable.
- **“Defend two fronts on a continuous world.”** Ring world uses the torus as the ring itself. The failure is a severed belt, merged coasts, or a start on an offshore fragment.

- **“Leave the walls to take the country.”** The Glacis (revision 2) makes the courtyard hold a small town and its gardens, puts the wealth (forest, fields, contested fords with orchards and quarries) out in the country, and leaves a bare, buildable glacis ring between: every forward move is made in the open beside the enemy's works. The failure is a courtyard too small to grow (its first trace), or a glacis crops can reach (sand at the covered way and the foot, proved by a grass flood).
- **“Expand one oasis at a time.”** Caravanserai (revision 2) makes sand unbuildable and crossable, so every forward building stands on an oasis and the prizes stand in walled caravanserais between neighbours. The failure is a home too poor to reach the first oasis (its revision 1 had 20 tiles of wood a colony), or oases so close that the desert stops mattering.

A theme is not a starting condition. The revision 1 Glacis, Allotments and Caravanserai were built around premade bases and drew the terrain as plumbing for them: one module on a lattice, a plain, lanes or circles between. They read as graph paper and circles and gave no AI anything to grow into; the rebuilds kept the three themes and designed the ground (see [the playbook](tuning-playbook.md) for the numbers).

Treat names such as “mountain,” “sunken terrace,” and “city block” as presentation metaphors: these examples implement stone masks, water, grass, sand and routes, rather than adding physical elevation or line-of-sight cover.

## Space, growth, and AI: compose constraints together

The repeated failure is not “too many resource tiles” in isolation. It is **renewable crops covering the same tiles needed for buildings and travel**. A sparse initial planting can still spread across a fertile home. Clearing a path once at generation protects the opening state only.

Use the three complementary functions of [Farmland](../../../../src/map/generator/shared/Farmland.h): cap a crop area with sand to contain spread; bridge both water rows and crop rows to give workers short cross-routes; reserve a grass construction plot within a sand ring. The default `FarmPlot` is 10×4 grass with a two-vertex ring. Canals uses smaller 4×4 pads for specific block kinds. Size the full surrounding terrain, not just the empty rectangle, and inspect the finished pure-grass footprint.

Anthill exposes a subtle interaction: a road through a pond's chamber prevents crops on one side from colonizing the others. Its implemented repair plants eight seeds around the pond. **Recommendation:** whenever roads partition a farm, seed each intended renewable pocket and check harvesting access independently. A growth barrier that protects the AI can also starve a field if seeded carelessly.

Old town deliberately leaves grass streets that buildings can close, whereas Anthill, Maze and Spider web default to sand routes. **Recommendation:** make this an explicit play-style decision. If an AI is expected to preserve a route that remains buildable, prove that in a game rather than assuming the AI understands the map's intended street plan.

Room counts in [Room](../../../../src/map/generator/shared/Room.h) are **overlapping building anchors**, not that many simultaneously placeable buildings. Anthill's `queen-room` target therefore needs interpretation; it does not promise sixty separate buildings. Check useful arrangements of the opening buildings, access around them and future expansion, in addition to the metric. Old town's two-block home is a concrete example of fixing the geometry instead of relaxing the settlement check.

Farm fairness includes orientation. `bestFarmRows` and `farmYield` encode the loss from diagonal beach rasterization: equal area at different angles need not produce equal food. Carousel and Switchbacks grow fields by yield before fitting rows. Both then validate that at least 95% of crop-row grass, and the entire construction plot, can be reached **after crops are clear**; that check does not establish present harvesting access or successful AI clearing.

## One home, stamped by quarter turns

A home that must be identical for every colony but is too detailed for an orbit of the whole map (a star fort, a village with its plot block, an oasis town) is designed once as a stencil in its own frame and stamped at every lattice site turned by a quarter turn drawn once per map. `turnStencilVertex`, `turnStencilTile` and `turnStencilPoint` (`Orbits.h`) carry it exactly, provided each grid is evaluated and turned by its own rule: a corner (undermap vertex) at offset (dx, dy) is the frame point (dx, dy) and turns about the origin vertex; a tile is the frame point (dx + 0.5, dy + 0.5) and lands one tile over on the axes the turn flips. Evaluate terrain at corners and kinds (wall, garden, town) at tile centres, turn each with its own function, and the stamped terrain and masks agree at every facing (The Glacis, Allotments, Caravanserai). Compute the stencil's own budgets (garden pure grass after beaches, town build sites, swarm site) inside the stencil, where they are cheap and exactly the same for every colony, and refuse or fall back before stamping.

Two cautions from their first tournaments:

- **Identical tiles are not identical for an AI.** Numbi's food estimate and building search run along the map's axes, so the same home can work in one facing and fail in the others (see the playbook's per-AI list). Draw the facing once per map, not per colony, so every home is an exact translation of the others and every AI meets the same home at every start; vary the facing between maps for variety. Design the home so its opening food is a compact block beside the swarm, which also helps whichever facing the map drew. The three rebuilds first drew a facing per colony; The Glacis' Numbi colonies reached 60 units in one facing and 20 to 30 in the others.
- **Choose the swarm site from the stencil, not an offset.** Put it on the tile farthest from any other kind of ground (Caravanserai), or where the design has budgeted open ground on every side, and leave at least three clear tiles round it.

## Derive features from the finished terrain

When a pattern is drawn over irregular regions (plots over warped parcels, gardens behind a slanted wall), do not compute which tiles belong to which feature from the pattern's arithmetic. Allotments' first rebuild labelled plots by band and strip index, and wherever a warped lane cut the pattern short, a patch of grass fell between labels and joined two plots or a plot to open ground. Lay the pattern's corners, lay the beaches, then take every eight-connected patch of pure grass in the region as one feature and give it its draws; slivers under a few tiles become bare. The validator can then prove containment with one labelled flood: no patch's grass reaches another patch or unlabelled grass.

## Sealing a sand line against a slanted wall

A sand line that closes a garden or a plot from a town must meet the wall without a diagonal gap, but a wall tile's stone needs four grass corners, so the line's corners cannot go on the wall. Stop the line at the wall's inner face (skip any corner touching a wall tile), then flood the would-be pure grass from the closed side over tiles that are not wall and convert any non-garden tile that touches the garden into wall, until nothing does (The Glacis). It costs a stone or two where the line meets a slant and seals exactly. Validate with a grass flood that treats stone as closed (`isGrass` and not a stone deposit); `cropSpreadEnvelope` ignores deposits and would walk through the wall.

## Contrast makes a feature read

A feature is recognisable by what surrounds it. The Glacis' cleared ring was invisible on a plain of grass and became the map's signature once woods were biased to crowd up to its sand foot. A desert stops being flat sand when dune bands carry scrub in their hollows (single grass corners, never two adjacent, so no buildable tile appears), rock mesas stand on a relief noise and scrub thickens into a halo round every oasis. Plots read as allotments when neighbouring plots are tended differently. When a preview does not read, add the surroundings before enlarging the feature.

## Boundaries and routes need two opposite proofs

For a designed arena, test **the route exists** and **no forbidden shortcut exists**. [Walls](../../../../src/map/generator/shared/Walls.h) contains both kinds of tools:

- `labelBorders` uses all eight neighbours. A cardinal-only diagonal stone line can leak.
- `designedStone` reports intended wall tiles that cannot hold stone because terrain is not pure grass. Do not silently ignore gaps in a wall that defines the concept.
- `seaMargin`, `sealCoasts` and `seaEntry` model the beach a swimmer can land on and whether it connects inland. Exclude designed sand roads and plot rings from the beach classification so they do not carry a coast wall through the base.
- `pieceLeak` closes specified gates and treats temporary crops/buildings as the ground they may later leave. This tests the permanent layout rather than relying on temporary obstructions to hide a leak.
- `towerReach` tests a whole 2×2 tower footprint against targets with the game's square range. A thin wall stops walking, never fire. Its array indices are zero-based: `kTowerRange[1] == 7` is the second player-facing tower level; do not repeat comments that ambiguously call this “level 1.”

Switchbacks checks both all-trails-closed and only-middle-legs-closed. Maze checks allowed cell adjacencies at junctions. Ring world tracks winding, because a connected U-shaped belt does not prove a complete ring. Choose an invariant that matches the intended tactic rather than applying one generic flood to every design.

[Roads](../../../../src/map/generator/shared/Roads.h) makes repair costs explicit. A generator may allow clearing ambient crops, while disallowing designed stone and water; Fingerprint intentionally permits a rock gap or a ford, but City states must protect its coast walls and causeways. **Recommendation:** name and preserve separate masks for structural stone and optional quarries. Recheck the design after every late repair, and inspect how much was altered. A successful return from a repair routine is not proof that the original design survived.

[Towers](../../../../src/map/generator/shared/Towers.h) treats future pads as occupied when deciding whether a proposed tower plan would block required routes, then reduces counts equally across colonies. It also excludes enemy swarms from range. Follow this principle for any preplaced structure: reserve its future space and its traffic effects before planting resources.

## Variety without dissolving the concept

Use more than global rotation. A rotated identical map gives a different screenshot while leaving the same decisions every game. The current generators demonstrate several useful levels:

1. **Topology:** Maze and Anthill reroll spanning trees and loops; Canals rerolls extra bridges; Spider web tears corresponding strands. Keep a guaranteed backbone, then add optional alternatives.
2. **Room or district roles:** Canals deals fourteen weighted block kinds and independent facings. City states chooses one of four home kinds and one of five commons hearts. Distinct roles make scouting informative; geometry-only noise does not replace them.
3. **Shape:** periodic coastline harmonics, bounded cell warp, bowing, sag, rough radial outlines and winding paths retain recognizable overall forms. Keep mandatory mouths and crossings flatter or wider than decorative boundaries.
4. **Economy and rewards:** patches, split fields, orchard arrangement and resource amounts vary where to expand. Pair a pad with resources, a risky dead end with a reward, and a remote island with room to exploit it.
5. **Seat assignment:** `dealStarts` shuffles which colony receives which site. Keep paired data together: Old town shuffles plaza-and-annex pairs. This removes fixed player-number geography without claiming that unequal sites become equal.

For exact fairness, make each random decision once per orbit and stamp every image, as Symmetric arena does. For repeated wedges, key choices in local coordinates, as Spider web does for strand tears. For asymmetric maps, use bounded variations around comparable opening access, then measure the outcomes. City states' new outer archipelago is a useful caution: a symmetric-looking outer layer is not necessarily symmetric under the player-home transformations.

**Recommendation:** expose a small number of controls with clear tactical meanings and retain enough seeded variation within each setting. Add controls for kinds with `choice`, binary features with `toggle`, and amounts with `percentage`; read their actual current IDs from definitions. Maze now uses percentage amount controls, despite older prose in the adding-a-generator guide describing it as an exception.

## Resource controls: learn from both successes and gaps

Many implementations scale ordinary fields with `scaledCount` or `scaledShare` while deliberately leaving starter guarantees and structural stone unchanged. This distinction preserves playability at 0% and keeps an arena's walls from disappearing with its quarry setting.

Do not assume every existing special layer already obeys a full percentage response. The reviewed code contains deliberate exceptions and potential gaps:

- City states' `ForestHeart` stamps its wood band directly; that band is not scaled by `wood-amount`. Several special orchard/islet/clump branches there and in Canals, Spider web and other shaped generators only test `scaledCount(1, amount) > 0`, leaving the surviving clump at a fixed size.
- Old town's stone is structural, and its `stone-amount` has no ambient quarry layer to scale. Carousel and Switchbacks similarly derive their useful stone primarily from structural walls.
- Ring world explicitly keeps each resource-island prize unchanged while scaling ambient resources. Symmetric arena deliberately keeps at least three fruit orbits even at 0% so every fruit remains, and scales fruit only up to 100%.
- City states and Spider web do not currently call the general `reopenCrampedStarts` backstop in their generation pipelines; they rely on their own clearance/route measures. A guide must not claim that every generator automatically receives that repair.

**Recommendation for new code:** inventory every resource-writing branch, classify it as a documented minimum, structural terrain, or scalable supply, and make every scalable supply respond to the corresponding shared amount. Test several intermediate amounts as well as 0/100/300; a presence test alone is not continuous quantity tuning. A control with no responsive supply is misleading: avoid adding it merely because another generator has it. Cap or spread additional deposits in ways that preserve building sites and route widths. Do not silently turn a nominal “amount” control into an undocumented layout switch.

## Robust geometry and shared fixes

Good robustness usually comes from budgeting before stamping:

- **Worst-case reach:** Contested commons reserves room using the radial shape's possible bulge, rather than the ideal circle. Ring world shares one displacement budget between width swing and rough coast, preserving its dry spine and minimum ocean simultaneously.
- **Full footprint:** Maze subtracts stone, water and beach width from the shortest warped edge before allowing the request. Canals warps while keeping opposite boundaries apart and centres clear enough for features.
- **Bounded search with useful fallback:** Ring world's start search widens from 12% to 25% to 50% of a slot. Amphitheatre switches all colonies together from paired bays to a far bay if necessary. Canals replaces a moat that cannot fit with a lake and omits walls that split the block.
- **Minimum useful feature:** Switchbacks checks that every farm reaches a meaningful area before filling leftovers; the geometry must fit at least two zigzag legs. Symmetric arena rejects settings without enough orchard orbits rather than dropping a required fruit.
- **Test the finished world:** beaches, late deposits, roads and towers can invalidate a correct abstract graph. Validators should use the finished map and reconstruct only the intended layout from a fresh context.

Move reusable fixes into the shared toolkit. The source history documents why this matters: Isles' repeated bridge-line tiles exposed a queue overrun in shared distance computation; Contested commons' whole-region spread needed a better bounded search; Amphitheatre moved from noisy turn-taking territory growth to weighted-distance balancing for smooth borders; farm growth needed to remove narrow fingers that later coast walls would seal. These are reusable concepts, not reasons to accumulate independent flood, beach, plot or wall implementations inside each new generator.

Use [Adding a generator](../../../../docs/map-generators/ADDING_A_GENERATOR.md) for the current module contract and registry steps. Its preferred shape is a named options struct, pure request-and-stream `design`, staged world mutation, and request/final-world checks, with comments explaining the game rule behind each important width, spacing or fallback. Copy the structure and reasoning style; do not copy stale comments, resource-scaling omissions, legacy dispatch patterns, or unexplained constants.

## What playtesting changed

The dated feedback embedded in the sources is unusually valuable evidence about *failure modes*, but it is not validation of a new design:

- Anthill: too little water, too little room, AI buildings sealing tunnels, and crops failing to seed across sand roads led to larger chambers, protected tunnels and multi-side pond planting.
- Canals: too many empty blocks led to useful district types; empty construction pads became farm hubs with nearby crops; forts and other built kinds made territory worth contesting.
- Old town: unequal access to outer farms led to removal of the perimeter wall, more protected farm plots, less wood and larger fountains.
- Carousel and Switchbacks: redundant kits and crops obscuring central fruit objectives led to leaner homes and fruit-only centres; farm layout was revised to use space without destroying the map's readability.
- City states: an uninteresting barren-home variant was removed, and empty wrap-space became islands large enough to host useful building plots.

**Recommendation:** a new generator is ready only when its intended choices happen in actual games. Watch workers leave the swarm, bring wood to the first buildings, maintain food, reach farms and expand after growth; then watch warriors assemble, pass choke points and reach a worthwhile objective. Review late growth and building blockades, not just minute-zero previews. Preserve the seed, settings, map, relevant screenshots and replay/game observations for each failed and repaired case.
