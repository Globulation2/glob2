# From map concept to playable generator

Treat a map as an economy and a travel network that evolve during play. Its silhouette is only one part of the design. Preserve the user's concept while finding geometry, resource policy and controls that make its intended decisions possible.

This skill is grounded in a code review of every built-in generator and in the tuning records of the ones rebuilt since. The references distinguish current mechanisms from design recommendations; they do not certify that every existing generator is fun or balanced. Paths below are relative to this skill. Follow repository `AGENTS.md`; recheck the code when implementing, because this guide is a source map, not a frozen engine specification.

## Turn the idea into a playable contract

Write a short design brief before coding, scaled to the request. It should answer:

- **What choice do players face?** Early pressure, contested farmland, several fronts, compact city expansion, clearing a forest, investing in swimming. Where and why do opponents meet?
- **What does every colony need?** Budget connected building footprints, worker access, renewable crops and exits separately, for food, wood, population, upgrades and an army.
- **What is home ground, expansion ground, and contested ground?** Describe the opening, first contact, and how the map changes as resources spread or units gain abilities.
- **What does the subject look like?** Name the three or four visual signatures a person would recognise it by in a photograph *before* naming any mechanism, and check the planned construction draws each one. Terraces built from contour rings never read as terraces; they had to be rebuilt as strips down whole hillsides ([model the real thing](references/tuning-playbook.md#the-picture-is-the-first-review)).
- **What makes the subject readable is contrast.** A cleared glacis is invisible on a plain of grass and reads against woods crowding up to it; a desert reads through dune bands, scrub hollows and rock, not flat sand. Plan the surroundings that make the signature stand out ([contrast](references/shaped-generators.md#contrast-makes-a-feature-read)).
- **Does the concept live in the landscape, or only in a starting condition?** A map built around how a colony starts (a premade base, a head start) leaves the terrain as plumbing for that start, reads as graph paper, and gives AIs nothing to grow into. Decide what the map is about after the opening, then design the opening to lead into it ([a theme is not a starting condition](references/shaped-generators.md#translate-a-visual-idea-into-a-game)).
- **Symmetric or natural, stamped homes or found starts?** Settle the construction's character with the maintainer before building, as a question with previews of each; it is the first thing they react to and the costliest to change.
- **When the concept is scarcity,** read the engine's cost tables first: which buildings and ammunition consume the resource at which level, whether any kit re-adds it, how fast one deposit yields, and which rules bind whatever *guards* the prize. Make the scarcity a validated promise, check every AI can find the prize at all, and measure the haul door to door ([a scarce resource as the prize](references/gameplay-and-playability.md#a-scarce-resource-as-the-prize), [a shared prize](references/tuning-playbook.md#a-shared-prize-the-ais-must-want-and-a-person-must-hold)).
- **Name the intended shortage, and fund the rest of the game.** A contested orchard still needs ordinary food for armies. Reachable starter deposits and a fairness score do not establish sustainable production ([survival versus a fighting economy](references/tuning-playbook.md#survival-is-not-a-fighting-economy)).
- **Which properties are invariants, which vary by seed, which are player controls?** State supported sizes, aspect ratios and colony counts; make unsupported combinations explicit.
- **Give topology words a scope.** Does "two crossings" mean globally, per colony, or per neighbour? Does "one entrance" survive swimming? Resolve consequential ambiguity before encoding the wrong route graph.
- **How will the design fail in practice, and what observation would trigger a revision?** A crop belt engulfs homes, a tower covers the only attack route, swimming removes the intended front, equal starts lead to unequal expansion.

Then choose the construction family by the play contract. Recognisable shapes use transformed masks and repeated home modules; landscapes use fields, regions and scored start placement; arenas carve connected rooms and fronts from impassable ground. Hybrids are useful. A *solved* map states its properties as numbers and searches for ground that satisfies them, which is worth it only for decisions construction genuinely cannot reach ([solved maps](references/constraint-solving.md)). Do not force an asymmetric landscape into exact symmetry because it is easier to validate.

## Design and implementation loop

1. **Budget geometry before decoration.** Fit homes, complete building footprints and upgrade room, sustainable crop plots, gathering edges, the intended routes, beaches and boundary thickness on the smallest supported map. Use toroidal distances and account for rasterization; a large grass count does not imply buildable space. On detached islands, budget disjoint service footprints per assigned colony ([island capacity](references/gameplay-and-playability.md#budget-detached-islands-per-assigned-colony)). Negotiate the layout in the robust order: room per feature, optional features dropped first, homes shrunk only to a documented floor, then refuse naming the control ([budget before stamping](references/tuning-playbook.md#budget-before-stamping-refuse-before-shrinking-below-a-floor)).

2. **Control the future resource footprint.** Separate guaranteed starter supplies, renewable farmland, ambient abundance and structural deposits. Water placement, dry ground and sand containment are the only tools: **no-growth zones are forbidden on generated maps** (`validateGeneratedWorld` refuses any generated world with one; the saved `canResourcesGrow` flag is for hand-made scenarios). Plant kits before ambient layers and protect them from every clearing pass. Wood spreads without wheat's one-in-three gate, so a scatter of copses on fertile ground is a forest with a delay ([overgrowth](references/gameplay-and-playability.md#wood-overgrowth-the-failure-a-preview-cannot-show)). For an inn court, verify the inn footprint, crop adjacency, worker access and AI discovery, not just legal placement ([opening courts](references/tuning-playbook.md#an-opening-court-must-be-legal-reachable-and-discovered)).

3. **Choose an honest fairness model** and validate what it promises: construction (exact orbits or one stencil stamped by quarter turns), search (arrangements scored on the finished layout, or a walk band round a shared objective), or measurement (the lobby's best-of-five). Independently rasterized wedges cannot prove symmetry; anything placed after site selection changes the walks it balanced; a *searched* fairness score is the weakest evidence of all, because the search drove the number it was given. Deal interchangeable sites to team indices before indexing any per-team array; preserve explicit roles such as a colony-zero capital. Draw a stencil's facing once per map, since an axis-scanning AI meets a turned home differently. Record every per-colony design draw in telemetry ([fairness models](references/tuning-playbook.md#fair-by-construction-fair-by-search-or-fair-by-measurement), [stamped homes](references/shaped-generators.md#one-home-stamped-by-quarter-turns), [optimise what decides games](references/constraint-solving.md#optimise-what-decides-games-not-what-is-easy-to-measure)).

4. **Compose shared stages.** Keep the layout in a reconstructible `design(request, context)`, stamp terrain, settle colonies, furnish resources, repair only permitted failures, and validate the finished world. Inspect `shared/` before inventing a helper; extract a reusable routine on its second use; fix primitive bugs at the primitive with regressions and account for every affected generator's golden rows ([compose mechanisms](references/implementation-and-verification.md#compose-mechanisms-instead-of-copying-recipes)).

5. **Make variation strategic, and give every hard-coded shape several designs.** Change routes, field locations, district shapes, crossings or objectives within the concept's invariants, using named seed streams; resource sprite changes and shuffled team labels are not replay variety. A map whose every module is the same stamp reads as monotonous even when it plays well: build a few designs that meet the same contract, choose one per map from its own stream, give colonies with the same role the same design, and pin each design against the previous release across sizes and colony counts before shipping; the weakest design sets the success rate. Keep the variation inside the concept's look and its fairness model ([variety without dissolving the concept](references/shaped-generators.md#variety-without-dissolving-the-concept), [variety without destroying the concept](references/landscape-generators.md#variety-without-destroying-the-concept)).

6. **Expose meaningful controls and catalog tags.** Controls should change a player decision (crossing width, room allocation). Wire every ambient resource layer to its registered amount; keep starter guarantees and structural walls invariant and document them; test both 0% and crowding. Tag the generator for the landscape picker (a terrain, each searchable feature, a style, and a fairness tag only when fair by construction); the registry refuses a playable generator without tags ([controls and resource policy](references/implementation-and-verification.md#controls-and-resource-policy), [adding a generator](../../../docs/map-generators/ADDING_A_GENERATOR.md)).

7. **Instrument internal decisions.** Record effective variants, requested/placed features, calibration inputs and outputs, and actual fallback branches per the [telemetry guide](../../../docs/map-generators/TELEMETRY.md). Telemetry adds no RNG draws, grid scans or expensive analysis, and collection stays off outside requested JSON output. Keep stable, bounded metrics; remove per-tile debug traces ([internal telemetry](references/implementation-and-verification.md#internal-telemetry-is-part-of-implementation-and-tuning)).

8. **Verify in the playbook's order, and revise.** Rename streams, telemetry keys and controls *before* any measurement pass, since a stream's name is part of every draw ([stream names](references/constraint-solving.md#a-stream-name-is-part-of-every-draw)). Then:
   - **Look first.** Show previews as a maintainer will: does it read as the concept, with nothing invented where it claims reality and nothing drawn with compasses where it claims nature? **Maps can't be ugly**: a playability fix that destroys the picture is not finished ([the picture is the first review](references/tuning-playbook.md#the-picture-is-the-first-review)).
   - **Measure every control on its own, roll everything at random, run a reliability pass** of extremes and a couple of thousand rolls; state per control what should move before reading the table ([show, measure, roll](references/tuning-playbook.md#show-the-map-then-measure-the-knobs-then-roll-everything)). Sweep every shape after every geometry change; time the largest map beside a comparable generator from the first working build.
   - **Measure food as growth potential** within 24 and 48 steps of each home beside a reference generator, and count wheat within 12 tiles of the swarm on every revision; a map with rivers and lakes can still starve ([growth potential](references/tuning-playbook.md#measure-growth-potential-not-water-share)).
   - **Calibrate locally**: a 20,000–30,000-tick four-AI game beside the same seed on a reference generator (Forts, Hedgerow Country) shows in minutes whether the economy is food-capped ([calibrate locally](references/tuning-playbook.md#calibrate-locally-before-a-tournament)). When the concept is a mechanism, prove it with its own counters against the same game with the mechanism off, before polishing the look.
   - **Play a rotation tournament** with the newer AIs only (Nicowar, Cortex, Cabino, Maxima; never tune for Numbi, Castor or Warrush), read the per-start economy before the win counts, and name the shape of the result before choosing a remedy ([which AIs](references/tuning-playbook.md#which-ais-to-play), [the loop](references/tuning-playbook.md#the-measurement-loop), [four shapes](references/tuning-playbook.md#four-shapes-a-tournament-result-takes)). Preserve intentional roles and alliances on asymmetric maps. Pin the design variant for paired runs; single games vary by up to 40%.
   - **Diagnose before changing the economy**: a stall despite nearby resources may be construction origin ([stalled builder](references/tuning-playbook.md#a-stalled-builder-can-look-like-a-resource-shortage)); swimming progression needs local pools proved ([swimming access](references/tuning-playbook.md#prove-local-swimming-access-not-just-a-swimming-flag)); a common shortage on several maps does not justify one shared remedy ([distinct remedies](references/tuning-playbook.md#distinct-maps-distinct-remedies)). Test a remedy through an existing control before writing code. Tune on one seed set and check another; retain failing seeds. Look at a finished game, not only a preview.
   - **Ask a fresh agent to review it** against this skill, in rounds, on a frozen copy of the build, before calling it done ([independent review](references/tuning-playbook.md#ask-an-independent-reviewer-in-rounds)).

## Completion evidence

Report the intended play experience, changed controls and primitives, supported parameter envelope, reproducible commands and artifact locations, and observed limits. Include maps with request JSON, previews, sweep results, internal telemetry and its bulk analysis, generation timing comparisons, and game evidence proportional to the change. Distinguish generation repeatability, static fairness, AI performance and human fun. Follow the repository's compatibility and review rules for changes extending into simulation or existing gameplay; do not claim unperformed platform comparisons or playtests.
