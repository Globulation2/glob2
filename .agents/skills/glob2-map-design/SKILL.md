# From map concept to playable generator

Treat a map as an economy and a travel network that evolve during play. Its silhouette is only one part of the design. Preserve the user's concept while finding geometry, resource policy, and controls that make its intended decisions possible.

This skill is grounded in a code/comment review of all 49 built-in generators. The references distinguish current mechanisms from design recommendations; they do not certify that every existing generator is fun or balanced. Paths below are relative to this skill. Follow repository `AGENTS.md`; recheck relevant code when implementing because this guide is a source map, not a frozen engine specification.

## Turn the idea into a playable contract

Write a short design brief before coding, scaled to the request:

- What choice should players face: early pressure, contested farmland, several fronts, compact city expansion, clearing a forest, or investing in swimming? Where and why do opponents meet?
- What does every colony need to establish food, wood, population, upgrades, and an army? Budget connected building footprints, worker access, renewable crops, and exits separately.
- What is home ground, what is expansion ground, and what is worth contesting? Describe the opening, first contact, and later changes as resources spread or units gain abilities.
- What does the subject look like? Name the three or four visual signatures a person would recognise it by in a photograph, before naming any mechanism, and check the planned construction draws each one. Rice terraces built from the nearest primitive (contour rings round a summit) never read as rice terraces however they were decorated; they had to be rebuilt as long winding strips down whole hillsides, and the ring map became Hills (see "model the real thing" in [the tuning playbook](references/tuning-playbook.md)).
- Does the concept live in the landscape, or only in a starting condition? A map built around how a colony starts (a premade base, a big garrison, a head start) leaves the terrain as plumbing for that start: The Glacis, Allotments and Caravanserai started finished or staked-out bases on a lattice of identical modules, read as graph paper and circles, and gave AIs nothing to grow into (Nicowar bred 14 to 15 times a colony from its premade 48; Caravanserai starved out two colonies in three). Their rebuilds (2026-09-16) kept each theme and put it in the ground. Decide what the map is about after the opening, then design the opening to lead into it.
- What makes the subject readable is contrast, not the feature alone. A cleared glacis is invisible on a plain of grass; it reads against the woods that crowd up to it. A desert reads through dune bands, scrub hollows and rock, not through flat sand; allotments read through narrow plots each tended differently, not through identical pads. Plan the surroundings that make the signature stand out.
- Settle the construction's character with the maintainer before building: symmetric or natural, stamped homes or found starts. They are the first thing a maintainer reacts to and the costliest to change: Central Quarry was planned as a symmetric moated keep with a home per wedge and rebuilt mid-build as a natural landscape after "avoid too much symmetry ... don't do a home plot". Ask it as a question with previews of each, not as an assumption.
- When the concept is scarcity (one resource everyone fights over), read the engine's tables before anything else: which buildings and which ammunition consume it at which level, whether any guarantee or ambient kit re-adds it, and how fast one deposit yields. Stone is eternal, so a six-tile quarry is limited by the workers who can stand beside it, level-0 buildings cost none of it, and towers need it only to fire. Then make the scarcity a validated promise (no stone off the isle), and make sure every AI can find the prize at all: Maxima counts stone only within 20 tiles of its own buildings and never mined a distant quarry. Read the rules that bind whatever *guards* the prize as well: Hidden Oasis sealed its algae with granted level-2 towers that no worker on the map was allowed to resupply (build levels come from schools, schools need algae), so its seal emptied and opened by itself for two review rounds. Measure the haul door to door, mechanism included: a fair 70 steps to a gorge's mouth plus an 85-step gorge put the prize out of every AI's reach.
- Which properties are invariants, which may vary by seed, and which are player controls? State the supported sizes, aspect ratios, and colony counts. Make unsupported combinations explicit.
- Give topology words a scope: does “two crossings” mean globally, per colony, or per neighbour? Does “one entrance” remain true after swimming? Resolve consequential ambiguity before encoding the wrong route graph.
- How will the design fail in practice, and what observation would cause a revision? Examples: a crop belt engulfs homes, a tower covers the only attack route, swimming removes the intended front, or equal starts lead to radically unequal expansion.

Choose the construction family by the play contract. Most maps are drawn; a *solved* map states its properties as measurable numbers and searches for ground that satisfies them, which is worth it only for decisions construction genuinely cannot reach ([solved maps](references/constraint-solving.md)). Recognizable shapes can use transformed masks and repeated home modules; landscapes can use fields, regions, and scored start placement; arenas can carve connected rooms and fronts from impassable ground. Hybrids are useful. Do not force an asymmetric landscape into exact symmetry merely because it is easier to validate.

## Read the references that bear on the design

- Read [gameplay and playability](references/gameplay-and-playability.md) when deciding terrain, resource sustainability, AI access, building room, fighting distances, or playtests. It explains why initially open maps can become unplayable.
- Read [shaped generators](references/shaped-generators.md) for arenas, cities, canals, repeated modules, rotational/translation symmetry, and variation inside a defined shape.
- Read [landscape generators](references/landscape-generators.md) for organic terrain, asymmetric starts, hydrology, resource bands, forests, and the older generators' useful lessons and limitations.
- Read [solved maps](references/constraint-solving.md) before pointing any search, annealer or constraint solver at a map, and when a map's fairness is to be *measured* rather than drawn or mirrored. It gives the three conditions a search has to meet to be worth writing, the difference between statistical targets a search can descend and topological ones it cannot, and the historical tournament that found stronger position bias in the terrain-searching generator, without establishing that either approach guarantees fairness.
- Read [the tuning playbook](references/tuning-playbook.md) before the first AI game and whenever a colony stalls, starves or never reaches a rival. It names the opening-economy, catchment, fairness-model and route-protection defects that AI games expose in otherwise valid maps, compares the remedies the shipped generators chose, and describes the rotation-tournament loop that measures them.
- Read [distributed telemetry studies](references/distributed-telemetry.md) for larger seed/settings matrices, SSH workers, recovery, and aggregate analysis of internal and final-map measurements. Use this workflow as part of generator telemetry validation before choosing defaults.
- Read [implementation and verification](references/implementation-and-verification.md) when selecting primitives, adding controls, registering code, repairing helpers, or planning evidence. It includes commands and explains what each check does and does not establish.
- Use the [scripts](scripts/): `render_terrain.py` (a close-up of the tiles the game sees, beaches and one-corner sand lines included), `game_economy.py` (one headless game's per-colony economy and chosen AI values), `tournament_starts.py` (a rotation tournament's per-start economy, optionally joined to a per-colony design draw), `sweep_shapes.sh` (every map shape and colony count, three seeds, refusals listed), `control_study.py` (any generator's one-control-at-a-time study and random rolls, natively in parallel, with dead steps flagged), `game_counters.py` (per-colony mechanism counters from `GLOB2_MEASURE`) and `growth_potential.c` (the engine's crop growth chance summed over the farmland each colony can walk to: food potential, not water share), `refusal_sweep.py` (one control over many seeds, reporting the refusal rate per value — how the top of a range gets found), `final_map_stats.py` (several generators' finished maps side by side at their defaults) and `contact_sheet.py` (a labelled grid of previews across settings or seeds). Each file's header gives the commands that produce its input.

Use the generator tables to select the closest precedents, then read their current headers, complete implementations, and relevant helper bodies. Compare the intended behavior in comments with what the final validator actually checks. Reuse a mechanism, not an entire generator's accidental constraints.

## Design and implementation loop

1. **Budget geometry before decoration.** Fit homes, complete building footprints and upgrade room, sustainable crop plots, gathering edges, multiple useful routes where intended, beaches, and boundary thickness on the smallest supported map. Use toroidal distances and account for rasterization. A large grass count does not imply buildable space.
2. **Control the future resource footprint.** Separate guaranteed starter supplies, renewable farmland, ambient abundance, and structural deposits. Use water placement, dry zones and sand containment deliberately — those are the only tools. Protect roads and expansion ground. **No-growth zones are forbidden on generated maps:** the engine's saved `canResourcesGrow` tile flag is for hand-made scenarios such as the tutorial, `validateGeneratedWorld` refuses any generated world with a single no-growth tile, and the toolkit has no helper for it. A service court an inn must stand on beside its wheat is held clear with one row of sand corners, which leaves the inn beside the grain and stops any crop extending across. More wheat is not automatically better for an AI.
3. **Choose an honest fairness model.** Exact tile orbits can prove symmetry; independently rasterized wedges cannot. A natural map around one shared objective can be fair by walk: spread sites by walking distance only inside a narrow band of walking distance from the objective, with a floor on the straight-line (swimming) distance and on the nearest rival, and validate those on the finished map. Anything placed after site selection changes the walks it balanced (a pond dug on the route, a trail wandering round a wood), so keep later features off each site's shortest routes and re-measure ([tuning playbook](references/tuning-playbook.md#fair-by-construction-fair-by-search-or-fair-by-measurement)). For asymmetric worlds, optimize actual post-settlement resource access and room, then inspect expansion and contact costs. Randomly deal sites to team indices before indexing kits or defenses. Equal starting scores are useful evidence, not proof of fair games — and a *searched* fairness score is the weakest evidence of all, because the search drove the number it was given. Even Ground optimises catchment spread to near zero, scores 0.894, and still loses eight games out of eight from one start; the colony fitness that actually predicts wins was never in its objective. If you optimise for fairness, optimise the measure that predicts winning and check it in a tournament ([solved maps](references/constraint-solving.md#optimise-what-decides-games-not-what-is-easy-to-measure)). A home designed once and stamped by quarter turns (`turnStencilTile`/`turnStencilVertex`, `Orbits.h`) is identical tile for tile, but not for an AI whose scans run along the map's axes: with a facing per colony, The Glacis' Numbi colonies reached 60 units in one facing and 15 to 30 in the others. Draw such a facing once per map, so homes are exact translations. Record every per-colony design draw (variant, neighbour) in telemetry and check a tournament's weak starts against it. A stencil stamped into a landscape that keeps drawing round it is identical only if it is stamped last over an erased footprint, with its crops sown in its own frame ([shaped generators](references/shaped-generators.md#stamping-a-stencil-into-a-living-landscape)).
4. **Compose shared stages.** Keep the layout in a reconstructible `design(request, context)`, stamp terrain, settle colonies, furnish resources, repair only permitted failures, and validate the finished world. Inspect `shared/` before inventing a helper; extract a reusable routine on its second use. Fix primitive bugs with regressions and account for every affected generator's output.
5. **Make variation strategic.** Change routes, field locations, district/cell shapes, crossings or objectives within the concept's invariants, using named seed streams. Mere resource sprite changes or shuffling team labels are insufficient replay variety. For exact symmetry, vary a fundamental domain or an invariant field and reproduce its orbits. **Variety is a basic rule of map making, in appearance as well as play:** a map whose every cell, field or module is the same stamp reads as monotonous even when it plays well. Give repeated elements several designs and patterns (Canals' varied blocks are the model), break up large uniform areas with small terrain features, and check a preview for sameness as well as for ugliness; keep the variation inside the concept's look ("Maps can't be ugly") and its fairness model. **A hard-coded shape comes in several designs.** When a generator stamps a specific drawn shape — a home module, a fort, a town plot, a garden, a pond — build a few designs that meet the same contract (and turns and mirrors where the concept allows), choose one per map from its own named stream, and give every colony that same design so the opening stays fair; neutral copies may draw their own. The same stamp on every map is too boring, however good it is. Canals' block kinds, City states' home kinds and commons hearts, Hedgerow Country's and Breachable Highlands' centrepieces, and the home designs of Forts, Lava shield, Sierpiński Gardens and Hilbert River are the models. Pin each design and compare it with the previous release across sizes and colony counts before shipping: the weakest design sets the generator's success rate ([shaped generators](references/shaped-generators.md#variety-without-dissolving-the-concept) has the method and the traps).
6. **Expose meaningful variants.** Tag the generator for the landscape picker too: several catalog tags in its definition (a terrain, every feature a player would search for, a style, and a fairness tag only when fair by construction), reusing existing values; the registry refuses a playable generator without them ([adding a generator](../../../docs/map-generators/ADDING_A_GENERATOR.md)). Controls should change a player decision, such as crossing width or room allocation. Wire every ambient resource layer to its applicable registered resource amount. Document guaranteed supplies and structural-wall exceptions. Test both scarcity and crowding: 0% may retain the starter guarantee, while high amounts must leave room and routes.
7. **Instrument and analyze internal decisions.** Use the [telemetry guide](../../../docs/map-generators/TELEMETRY.md) to record effective variants, requested/placed features, calibration inputs/outputs and actual fallback or omission branches. Reuse existing values and cheap counters; collection stays off outside requested JSON output, and telemetry must add no RNG draws, grid scans or expensive analysis. Keep stable, bounded metrics that explain many seeds; remove temporary per-tile/per-candidate debug traces. Bulk-generate a seed/settings matrix and join internal observations with final-map metrics, inspecting failures, missing records and rare variants before tuning. Benchmark generation with collection on and off.
8. **Verify progressively and revise.** First check structural invariants and final geometry, then seed/parameter sweeps and preview comparisons, then real AI games and human play. Rename streams, telemetry keys and controls **before** the measurement pass: a stream's name is mixed into every draw from it, so renaming one late moves every map and makes every figure you have recorded describe maps that no longer exist ([solved maps](references/constraint-solving.md#a-stream-name-is-part-of-every-draw)). Look at the preview as a maintainer will, before any number: does it read as the concept at a glance, with nothing invented where it claims reality and nothing drawn with compasses where it claims nature (the [playbook](references/tuning-playbook.md) lists the remarks first looks produce and the cheap answers)? Measure every control on its own, roll every control at random for the maintainer to eyeball, and run a reliability pass of extremes and a couple of thousand random rolls before games (the [playbook](references/tuning-playbook.md#show-the-map-then-measure-the-knobs-then-roll-everything)). Before calling a generator done, play a rotation tournament on it and read the per-start economy before the win counts: the [tuning playbook](references/tuning-playbook.md) names the shapes a result takes (a doomed start, a food-capped economy, a peaceful stalemate, an AI-specific collapse) and the remedy each calls for. When the concept is a mechanism (a seal, a siege, a prize), prove the mechanism in one game with its own counters ([`scripts/game_counters.py`](scripts/game_counters.py): harvested against delivered, shots fired, ammunition consumed, buildings lost) beside the same game with the mechanism switched off, *before* polishing the look: Hidden Oasis spent a review round on its picture while its towers had never fired a resupplied shot. Before the tournament, calibrate cheaply: a 20,000- to 30,000-tick four-AI game on your map beside the same game on a reference generator (Forts, Hedgerow Country) with the same seed shows in minutes whether the economy is food-capped, and the AI's own telemetry says why (the [playbook](references/tuning-playbook.md#calibrate-locally-before-a-tournament)). Play-test with the newer AIs only, Nicowar, Cortex, Cabino and Maxima, and read each separately, since each fails differently. Don't tune a map for the older Numbi, Castor or Warrush: a stall that only they show is a limit to record, or an AI bug to fix in the AI, never a reason to change the map (maintainer decision 2026-09-16; see [which AIs to play](references/tuning-playbook.md#which-ais-to-play)). Test a remedy through an existing control before writing code, and pin the affected variant so paired runs compare the same kind of map. Include late growth, expansion and alternate movement abilities. Tune on one seed set and check another; retain failing seeds. Revisit the design when a repair erases its identity: **maps can't be ugly**, and a change made for mobility or playability that destroys the map's aesthetic vision is not finished — find the version that keeps the picture (the [playbook](references/tuning-playbook.md#the-picture-is-the-first-review) records the cases). Look at a finished game, not only a preview: growth, overgrowth and shorelines laid after the beach pass only show up there. Time the largest map with the most colonies beside a comparable generator from the first working build, not at the end: Central Quarry took 10–16 s at 512 against 0.6–1.5 s for its neighbours until its request check stopped building the whole design and its site preferences were memoised ([implementation and verification](references/implementation-and-verification.md#debugging-contracts-and-release-housekeeping)). Measure food as growth potential within 24 and 48 steps of each home beside a reference generator, not as water on the map: a map with rivers and lakes can still starve its colonies ([measure growth potential](references/tuning-playbook.md#measure-growth-potential-not-water-share)). Count wheat within 12 tiles of the swarm too: moving a town for room can strand its kit while every other measure improves. Compare revisions with the design variant pinned, since lobby rolls pick different variants and single games vary by up to 40%. Before calling a generator done, ask a fresh agent with none of your context to review it against this skill, in rounds, on a frozen copy of the build ([independent review](references/tuning-playbook.md#ask-an-independent-reviewer-in-rounds)).

## Completion evidence

For generator work, report the intended play experience, changed controls/primitives, supported parameter envelope, reproducible commands and artifact locations, and observed limits. Include maps with request JSON, previews, relevant sweep results, internal telemetry and its bulk analysis, generation timing comparisons, and game evidence proportional to the change. Distinguish generation repeatability, static fairness, AI performance and human fun. Follow the repository's compatibility and review rules for changes extending into simulation or existing gameplay; do not claim unperformed platform comparisons or playtests.
