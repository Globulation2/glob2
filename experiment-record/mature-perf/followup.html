Mature Oazis CPU investigation

Field propagation, preparation and building search account for 52.26% of observed CPU sample periods. Building logic accounts for 13.00%, shared AI runtime 10.66%, snapshots 5.91%, and executor functions 0.26%.

This favors reducing useful field/search work and its memory traffic as the next substantial optimization. The small scheduler simplification remains a separate experiment; this sample gives it little room to improve total CPU in this workload.

What was actually measured

One continuously evolved 256×256 Oazis game, 11 Nicowars, seed 1738462951, three compute participants including the owner, GPU compute off, six allocated logical CPUs. Sampling covered the observed late-game window near ticks 120,064–128,000. There were 8,986 task-clock samples and about 18.01 seconds of aggregate observed sample periods.

The profiling executable was relinked from the original release objects. Code and allocated runtime sections matched the accepted executable. A loaded 512-tick window matched per-tick world checksums; the continuous run matched final outcome/checksum and complete replay bytes. The continuous run does not establish every-tick late-game equivalence.

Next experiments

  1. Scalar relaxation stores: the compiled hot loop writes a cell and unused queue entry even when relaxation fails. A one-factor candidate skips those writes while retaining successful-entry order, costs, SIMD paths, continuation layers and publication timing. Added branches may regress performance, so it needs field oracles and paired native tests.
  2. Cached executor selection: reuse the wait predicate's selection while the same lock remains held. Test independently at sensible and wide allocations; retain priority and cancellation behavior.
  3. Resource profiles: central policy and frozen tournament allocations are being integrated. Requested intent and actual resolution are recorded. CPU, presentation and memory tier tuning remain pending evidence; no fixed three-thread policy has been adopted.

Evidence and limits

Corrected accounting · Independent reconciliation · Simulation qualification · Assembly-backed candidate

No observed loss or throttle records were found, and exclusive periods reconcile. Empty callchains occurred in 56.87% of samples; leaf ownership remains available, but complete caller attribution and full CPU-active-thread coverage are not established. This is a statistical ranking for one late-game workload on one host, not a whole-game CPU fraction, measured speedup, GPU measurement or historical causal attribution.
